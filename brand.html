Sprout brand guide
The brand
Sprout · what shipped, and why

This page began as a workbench: twelve open questions with the options laid side by side, built so the choices could be made by looking rather than by description. All twelve are now settled and in the app. What follows is the record of what they settled on.

It is set in Sprout's own stylesheet rather than a copy of it, so every swatch, pill and button below is the shipped rule rendering itself. Switch the theme at the top and the whole page changes with the same cascade the app uses.

01

The mark

Three paths: a stem, a lower leaf turning left, an upper leaf turning right. Drawn once, on a 100×100 artboard, and used everywhere without redrawing.

There used to be four drawings of this idea — two sprigs at different proportions, an unrelated leaf, and a symmetric sprout — so the browser tab, the home screen and the app interior each showed a different plant. A mark that changes between placements is not a mark; it is four pictures of the same subject.

Stroke weight is 5% of the artboard at 32px and above, and 7% below it. A hairline does not survive being scaled down any better than small type does — which is the same reasoning the display face takes a heavier weight for below 28px.

The mark is emerald on the deep field, and takes currentColor everywhere it can. The favicon and touch icon hard-code #3FBE8B on #0A1410 because a data URI cannot inherit; every other instance inherits, so it follows the theme.

Where it lives. The favicon and the apple-touch icon are inline data URIs in index.html — a zero-build app opened from the filesystem cannot rely on a separate request. The sidebar copy is .brand-glyph in the same file, and UI.icon('mark') in js/ui.js serves the interior. Four places, one geometry. Changing the drawing means changing all four, and bumping CACHE in sw.js.
The touch icon is square on purpose. The favicon carries rx="24" because a browser tab does not round it for you; iOS masks the home-screen icon itself, and a pre-rounded square inside that mask loses its corners twice.
02

Wordmark

SPROUT, always in capitals, always in the display face, always tracked open. Never set in the body serif, never in sentence case, never hyphenated across a line.

Splash — PP Hatton 200, 0.22em
Sprout
evergreen
Sidebar — PP Hatton 500, 0.2em, 23px
Sprout

Tracking is always applied as a matching text-indent. Letter-spacing adds a trailing gap after the last letter, which pulls a centred wordmark visibly left. The indent puts it back.

“evergreen” appears on the splash and nowhere else. It is the product introducing itself, in the script face, once per session. It used to sit under the sidebar wordmark on every screen for the whole session — at which point a flourish stops being a flourish and becomes furniture. The <title> keeps it, because a browser tab is also an introduction.

03

Colour

Two themes, both shipped, both first-class. Viridium is a near-black green field; Conservatory is warm ivory. Neither is a derivative of the other — the accent hues are re-picked per theme so they hold their contrast on the ground they actually sit on.

With nothing stored, the app opens Viridium, whatever the device asks for. It used to take the device's prefers-color-scheme, which meant a reader arriving from the near-black landing page could land in an ivory app — one product introducing itself twice. Sprout is dark-first everywhere a first-time reader meets it, so that is where it starts. Once the toggle has been used, that choice is the user's and outranks everything for good.

Every swatch below reads its hex from the live token. Switch the theme at the top of the page and the values re-read themselves. Nothing here is transcribed.

The three warm hues

“Lit” and “glow” invert on ivory, on purpose. In Viridium a -lit token is the lighter cut of its hue, because it has to separate from a near-black ground. In Conservatory it is the darker cut — --gold-lit is #7E6226 against --gold at #9C7C35. The suffix names the job, which is “the one that reads as text”, not the direction. Likewise --emerald-glow and --emerald resolve to the same value on ivory: a glow has nothing to glow against, so it settles back onto the solid. Neither is a copy-paste slip.
Oxblood does not change between themes. #6E2320 in both. It is the one hue that is never text and never a hairline — it only ever appears as a filled plate with --blood-ink on top, and a fill carries its own ground, so it has no lighter ivory variant to need.
04

What the colours mean

There are two ladders, and they are deliberately built from different materials so they cannot be mistaken for one another.

The urgency ladder — hairline and wash

One continuous warm ramp away from the house green. It answers “how soon”, and every rung is a hairline chip or a bead, never a solid block.

Watering beads
Watered Due soon Due today Overdue
The ramp used to end in pink. Which broke it at the one point that matters: “you have forgotten this plant” is where the eye most needs to land, and a hue change there reads as a different kind of state rather than a further one along the same scale. Terracotta is the same journey continued.

The hazard ladder — fill

Toxicity and destruction answer “what happens if this goes wrong”, which is not a matter of timing at all. The top rung is the only filled plate in the system.

Toxicity, as rendered by LOOKUPS.TOX
Cats: Pet safe Cats: Mildly toxic Cats: Toxic Cats: Highly toxic

Oxblood is the top rung only. Twenty-two of the forty-eight species are toxic to cats and exactly one is very-toxic. Putting the filled chip on both would mark half the catalogue, and a warning carried by half a catalogue stops being read. The one species that qualifies does so for cardiac glycosides rather than a sore mouth — which is the distinction the rung exists to make.

Oxblood is never text and never a hairline. Not a preference — a measurement. #6E2320 as text on the Viridium page is a contrast ratio of 1.72:1, which is invisible. As a fill with --blood-ink on top it is 9.46:1. The colour and the form were never two decisions; a deep red only works filled.

Destructive actions take the same plate
Cascade note, learned the hard way. .btn-blood has the same specificity as .btn, so source order decides. Declared before .btn, the delete button renders in the emerald gradient — a green delete. It must stay declared after it in css/styles.css.

There is one yellow. The system carried --sun and --gold at once, two champagnes a few percent apart, neither of which meant anything the other did not. --sun is deleted. Reintroducing a second warm neutral means first saying what it means that champagne does not.

Task type never gets a colour. LOOKUPS.CARE once carried a color field naming a hue per kind of job. Nothing ever read it, which was lucky: colouring by kind of work would have competed with the colour that carries urgency, and urgency is the one thing the palette has to say first.

05

Type

Four faces, each with one job. PP Hatton carries the display capitals and is self-hosted from css/fonts/. Cormorant Garamond holds body copy, care sheets and figures. Jost takes the interface — labels, buttons, pills. Sacramento is the script, and appears on the splash only.

PP Hatton ships in two weights, and the size picks the weight: 200 at 28px and above, 500 below. The face spans 14px to 86px in this app. Hatton has no optical size axis, so the pair stands in for one — the light cut at display sizes where its thin strokes are the point, the medium cut wherever a hairline would be asked for less than a pixel of ink and antialiasing would spread it into pale grey.

Any rule naming --display must also name a weight. With two cuts installed and no variable axis, an unstated weight is resolved by the browser's guesswork, which is not the same guess in every browser.

Why the crossover exists at all. The display face was Italiana, which has exactly one weight. Its thin strokes are about a ninth of its cap height, so below 20px they asked for roughly one pixel of ink — which antialiasing spread across two rows at partial coverage, leaving a pale grey line beside a near-black stem. “ADD TO YOUR GREENHOUSE” lost the crossbars of its T, E and H; the T read as an I. That imposed a 20px floor on every display setting in the app, which is why .section-title had been exiled to the sans. Hatton Medium at 14px is clean, so the section titles rejoined the display face.

Tracking tightens as size grows. 0.23em at 14px, 0.055em at 66px. Capitals set tight at small sizes read as cramped body copy; set loose at display sizes they fall apart into individual letters. 06

Tiles

Most plants never get a photograph, so the tile — a tinted plate carrying the plant's initial — is the most-seen surface in the app. It has to work as a set, at four sizes, in both themes.

M
F
P
S
C
A

Six fixed tints, chosen per theme, assigned by name.hashCode % 6. Each is a two-stop linear gradient plus a corner sheen. The class is tmark-0 through tmark-5.

They used to be computed. A hash fed hue, and saturation and lightness were fixed — which meant the whole set sat on one lightness with a few degrees between neighbours, and on a phone a wall of tiles read as one colour. Six hand-picked pairs are further apart from each other than a continuous hash ever was, and they can be checked for contrast against the tile letter, which a generated colour cannot.

The tile letter is the one component where the type crossover lives inside a single element. It runs 15px in a room strip to 86px on a hero. The base rule is the small case at weight 500; the two large placements override to 200.

07

Photography

Two ratios, not three. A plant is a vertical object, and the app used to crop it three different ways depending on where it appeared — so whatever the owner framed, at least one placement fought it.

The two crops
4 : 5
1 : 1
PlacementRatioWhy
Plant hero4 : 5The one placement where the photograph is the subject rather than a thumbnail
Plant card4 : 5Same crop as the hero, so one photograph serves both without a second framing
Diary grid1 : 1A grid of square cells; the subject here is the set, not any one frame

Below 720px the hero is capped at max-width: 78% and centred. At 430px wide, a full-bleed 4:5 plate is 482px tall — the entire fold, before the plant's name has appeared. On a species page, where there is never a photograph, that is 482px of empty tint. Narrowing keeps the crop and gives the plate back its proportion to the page.

Landscape phones get 16:9. Under max-height: 520px the hero and the splash wordmark both shrink, because otherwise either one eats the whole fold.

Photos are downscaled to a 1000px long edge at 72% JPEG quality before storage. localStorage is about 5MB and it is the entire budget — state, theme and every photograph share it.
08

Voice

Sprout speaks as itself, in the first person, as a knowledgeable friend rather than a system. It uses the reader's name where it has one.

It did not always. The app ran three grammatical persons at once — “Shall I shorten…” in the nudges, “What should we call you?” in the welcome sheet, “What should Sprout call you?” in the profile — the last two being the same question asked on two screens in two voices. Committing to “I” is what resolved it.

The persona is zoned

ZoneVoiceWhere
ConversationFirst person, warmGreetings, nudges, empty states, form hints, confirmations, toasts
ReferencePlain, authoritative, no personaSpecies care data, diagnosis explanations and treatment steps, toxicity notes

A knowledgeable friend reads you the label straight when it matters. A cheerful voice explaining which crystals burn a cat's mouth is worse than no persona at all. When in doubt about a string, ask whether it is talking to the reader or telling them a fact — the second one takes no personality.

Rules that keep it from grating

A greeting is read every morning, and warmth that lands in week one can wear out by week three.

RuleBecause
No exclamation marks in anything that repeatsEnthusiasm on a loop reads as a machine performing enthusiasm
One contraction per line — not none, not threeNone is stiff; three is a performance of casualness
Never open two consecutive strings the same wayTwo “Looks like…” in a row is where the seams show
Never tell the reader offThe app is accountable, not them
Say “Sprout” only when introducing itselfEverywhere else it is “I”. A narrator who keeps using their own name is not in the room
British English throughoutMatches the existing copy
A syntax note that is not a voice note. Apostrophes inside single-quoted JS strings need escaping as \'. That is never a reason to write “I will” where “I'll” belongs — the stiffness is visible to the reader and the escape is not. Every contraction in the app was written as \' for this reason.

09

The decision record

The twelve questions this page was built to answer, and what each one settled on. Kept because the reasoning is the part that stops a decision being re-litigated from scratch in six months.

01
The yellows · defect
Two champagnes, --sun and --gold, sat a few percent apart and neither meant anything the other did not. --sun deleted; every class that wore it moved to --gold.
02
Urgency and hazard · structural
They were one ramp, so “overdue” and “toxic” competed for the same rung. Split into two ladders separated by form — urgency is hairline and wash, hazard is fill.
03
Conservatory · theme status
Was the ivory theme a real theme or a leftover? First-class, and now the first-run default on a device in light mode. Opening a near-black app on a phone at a sunny window — which is where a plant app is most often opened — was the app overriding a preference it had never been given a reason to override.
04
The mark · identity
Four drawings of one idea across the tab, the home screen and the app interior. One drawing, three paths, four placements, with stroke weight the only thing that varies.
05
“evergreen” · nomenclature
On every screen, or once? Splash and the browser tab only. A flourish that appears on every screen for a whole session is furniture.
06
Plant tiles · most-seen surface
A hash drove hue with fixed saturation and lightness, so a wall of tiles read as one colour. Six hand-picked tint pairs per theme; the hash now picks an index, not a hue.
07
Voice · register
Four registers set against the app's real strings. The field notebook — warm, specific, unhurried — which Decision 12 then took one step further.
08
Photo ratios · three in play
3:2, 4:5 and 1:1 meant whatever the owner framed, one placement fought it. 4:5 for the hero and the card, 1:1 for the diary grid. 3:2 is gone.
09
Which red · measured
#6E2320, as a fill and only as a fill. The measurement made the form part of the same decision: as text on the Viridium page it is 1.72:1, and as a plate with --blood-ink on top it is 9.46:1.
10
The display face · licensing
PP Hatton, self-hosted and subset. It replaced Italiana, whose single weight imposed a 20px floor on every display setting in the app. Licensed for commercial use through Befonts.
11
One weight or two · optical size
Two — 200 and 500, crossing over at 28px. The face runs 14px to 86px here and has no optical size axis; the pair stands in for one.
12
How far the voice moves · eleven real strings
Light trimmed, Firm stopped explaining, Persona committed to “I” — and won, zoned, so the reference surfaces stay plain.
