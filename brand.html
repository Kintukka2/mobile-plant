Decision 01 · The yellows

Decision 02 · Urgency and hazard

structural
02

A late watering and a poisoned cat are the same colour

Terracotta currently carries five unrelated meanings across two different axes.

Now

All five, at the weight the app gives them:

3 days overdue Act quickly Toxic Highly toxic

Time-based and harm-based facts are indistinguishable. Only highly toxic separates itself, and it does so by fill — which is the mechanism the proposal generalises.

Proposed

Three ways to make the split. The measurements in Decision 09 rule out one of them on their own.

i · Form only
3 days overdueToxic to cats
ii · Colour only — not available
3 days overdueToxic to cats

That chip is a deep red outline on the near-black ground — 1.72:1. It is in the page; you almost cannot see it. Deep red cannot outline on Viridium.

iii · Both
3 days overdueToxicHighly toxic

Urgency — recoverable, time-based. Always wash + hairline, never filled.

Water in 6 days Due today 3 days overdue

Irreversible harm — a poisoned animal, a deleted record. Always filled, always carries a glyph.

Toxic to cats Highly toxic
Highly toxic to cats

One filled chip anywhere on a screen means this cannot be undone. Counter-intuitively the filled deep red is quieter on this ground than today's outlined terracotta — 1.72:1 against 6.69:1 — so Delete becomes heavy rather than loud, which is what the stylesheet always asked for.

One caveat, stated plainly. Red against green is the most common colour-blindness axis, and this system already uses emerald for “fine”. Roughly one man in twelve cannot separate them reliably. The fill-and-glyph rule is what carries the meaning for them, which is why both beats colour alone rather than merely adding to it.

The call

Both. The measurements make the choice for us: a deep red only works filled, so taking the colour takes the form rule with it. The fifth hue is earned here because it marks a different kind of statement — irreversible rather than merely urgent — and it never appears on the same axis as the other four.

Decision 03 · Conservatory

theme status
03

Is the daylight theme a product, or a courtesy?

Use the toggle at the top of this page to judge it. Everything you have read so far re-renders.

Now
  • A token remap plus four override rules
  • Real care in the shadows and the segmented groove
  • No component was designed in it
  • The OS setting is ignored. A phone in light mode opens a near-black app

That last one is a live bug, not a preference: app.js reads localStorage only, and prefers-color-scheme is never consulted.

Proposed

Two changes, separable:

First load

Honour prefers-color-scheme when nothing is stored. An explicit choice still wins forever after.

Status

Promote to first-class: components may carry per-theme decisions where the ground genuinely demands one, rather than inheriting a remap and hoping.

The call

A plant app is opened at a sunny window. Respecting the OS setting is a bug fix either way; first-class status is the judgement.

Decision 06 · Plant tiles

most-seen surface
06

The hash promises identity and delivers near-identical squares

This is what a new user sees most of — every plant without a photo.

Now

Name hashed to 100–143°, 26% saturation, 11% lightness. Eight real species:

Forty-four hue steps compressed into a band that narrow, at that saturation and lightness, is a difference you have to be told about. The colour is also defined in ui.js, outside the token system.

Proposed

Six hand-picked tints, assigned by the same hash. Same mechanism, differences you can actually see — and the set lives in the tokens.

Still within the house greens, still low-saturation, still one material lit from one side. The band simply stops pretending to hold more information than it can.

The call

Keep the idea, fix the execution. A fixed set is legible, nameable and belongs in the design system rather than in a view helper.

Decision 07 · Voice

~1,500 strings
07

The app speaks in three grammatical persons

Not a matter of taste — the same question is asked two different ways on two screens.

Now
Shall I shorten watering intervals by 2 days across your greenhouse? first person singular · weather.js ×3
What should we call you? first person plural · welcome sheet
What should Sprout call you? third person · profile · the same question

Beneath that, the register splits three ways too: care sheets are long and clinical, interface copy is clipped and dry, greetings are warm. And it has a habit of telling you off — “Guessing here will only give you a confident wrong answer.”

Proposed

Three candidates, each writing the welcome paragraph and then the weather nudge — the place where “Shall I” currently lives.

A · The quiet expertsecond person · imperative

Tell Sprout what you grow and where it lives. It works out when each plant needs water — from the species' real requirements, adjusted for your pot, your light and the season.

Ten dry days ahead, averaging 26°C. Warm dry air pulls moisture from soil faster. Shorten watering by two days?

B · The field notebookcurrent voice, person fixed · tightened

A home for your houseplants. Tell Sprout what you have and where it lives, and it works out the watering — from what the species actually wants, not a fixed reminder.

Ten days without meaningful rain, and highs averaging 26°C. Soil will dry faster than usual. Shorten watering by two days?

C · The plain instrumentfunctional · no character

Add your plants and rooms. Sprout calculates watering intervals from species data, pot size, room light and season.

Forecast: 10 dry days, average high 26°C. Recommended: shorten watering intervals by 2 days.

All three drop I and we. Sprout is named only where the product genuinely has to be. The question mark stays — asking is right; sounding like a person asking is not.

The call

B. The existing voice is the app's best asset and C throws it away. Keep the character, fix the person, cut the length. Stage the rewrite: interface copy, then diagnosis, then the species data.

Decision 09 · Which red

measured
09

A fourth rung for what cannot be undone

Only if Decision 02 takes the colour. Every figure below is a WCAG contrast ratio against the real grounds.

The rule it creates

Four rungs, and the fourth is not simply “more urgent”. It marks a different kind of fact: this cannot be taken back. A poisoned animal and a deleted plant record belong together; an overdue watering does not.

Fine
#3FBE8B
Soon
#C8A96B
Urgent
#D08A62
Irreversible
#6E2320

The first three are a ramp and read as one. The fourth steps off it — darker than the ground it sits on rather than lighter, which is why it registers as a different class of thing rather than a fourth degree of the same one.

Where it is allowed
  • Toxicity, where the household actually keeps that animal
  • Delete and reset — anything with no undo
  • Filled only. Never an outline, never text, never a hairline
  • Never on the same row as terracotta doing urgency
Toxicity keeps all four rungs

Only the top one turns oxblood. The counts are from the real dataset, for cats:

RatingTreatmentSpecies
Pet safemint wash23
Mildly toxicchampagne wash2
Toxicterracotta wash22
Highly toxicoxblood, filled1

22 against 1. Put oxblood on toxic and it lands on 22 of 48 species — nearly half the catalogue, which is how a mark stops being read at all. The one species at highly toxic is Kalanchoe, and it is there because of cardiac glycosides that cause arrhythmias rather than a sore mouth. That is the distinction the top rung exists to make.

Delete, three ways
Today — outlined terracotta. 6.69:1 against the ground: the loudest of the three.
Oxblood, built like the primary — gradient and glow. Ivory text at 7.37–11.55:1, better than the green primary's own 3.34:1.
Same fill, no glow. The glow on the primary is a lift — an invitation. A destructive control arguably should not be inviting.

All three sit at 2.21:1 against the ground against the emerald primary's 5.33:1 — so an oxblood Delete is recessive on the page while being the most legible button in the app once you look at it. That is the combination the stylesheet asked for and never got.

Every candidate fails as text on the dark ground and every one succeeds as a fill. That is not a close call between options — it is the same verdict five times, and it is what makes “filled only” a measurement rather than a preference.

The call

Charcoal red measures marginally better and matches your description more literally, but at 37% saturation it reads as dark brown on a near-black green ground — and a warning colour that has to be explained is not doing its job. Oxblood keeps the earth in it while still landing as red.

Decision 10 · The display face

licensing
10

Hatton would fix a real defect — if it were licensed

Every specimen below is set at the app's own sizes, including the two where the current face fails.

The file you sent cannot be used as supplied. The fonts are PP Hatton, © 2019 Mathieu Desjardins, Pangram Pangram Foundry — a commercial release, marked “all rights reserved” in the font's own metadata.

The bundled Befonts-License.txt reads “Available for Personal & Commercial Use”, but Befonts is an aggregator, not the rights holder, and cannot grant rights to someone else's typeface. Shipping it would mean serving the binary publicly from the repo, which is redistribution.

Two honest routes. Buy a webfont licence from pangrampangram.com — it is a genuinely good face and the specimens below make the case — or take one of the free alternatives, which get closer than you might expect. The specimens are rendered images for evaluation, not embedded fonts.

The call
