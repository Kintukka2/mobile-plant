# Sprout — Greenhouse & Home Planner demo

Narration script, slide by slide. Presenter voice, not Sprout's — the app
speaks in the first person on screen, the narrator does not speak as it.

British English throughout. Anything in **bold quotes** is on-screen text
quoted verbatim, so the words spoken and the words shown do not disagree.

Run time as written: **3 min 30 s**. Drop slides 3 and 14 for a 3:03 cut, or
slides 3, 7, 9 and 14 for a 2:35 one. For a 30-second store preview, keep
only slides 6, 9 and 11 — trace, north, place — and lose the narration.

---

## Slide 1 — The premise (0:00–0:15)

**On screen:** Today, mid-morning. The greeting, the count, two or three
plants in the list.

> Most plant apps ask you how often you want reminding. That is a question
> about you, not about the plant — and it is the reason those reminders get
> dismissed by week three. Sprout never asks it. It works the interval out
> from the species, the pot, the season, and the light in the room the plant
> actually stands in.

**Note:** don't linger. This slide exists to set up the word *light*, which
is what the whole planner is for.

---

## Slide 2 — The greenhouse (0:15–0:35)

**On screen:** Greenhouse tab, **"Rooms"** selected. Room cards with their
plant count and light label.

> Your collection is a greenhouse, and a greenhouse is made of rooms. The
> mental model is a smart-home app — except you add plants instead of
> devices. Each room carries a light level and a window aspect, and every
> plant standing in it is scheduled against those.

Point at one card's light label — **"Bright indirect"**, **"Medium light"** —
and say the number out loud: *five rungs, from no natural light at all up to
direct sun.*

---

## Slide 3 — Where the reading comes from (0:35–0:50)

**On screen:** still the rooms list. Hover or tap the plan card at the top:
**"Update greenhouse"** / **"Map out your home using the planner"**.

> You can type that light level in yourself. Most people guess, and a guess
> here is expensive — it is the input every watering interval is built on.
> So there is a second way to get it, and it is the interesting one.

Tap through to the planner.

---

## Slide 4 — An empty grid (0:50–1:02)

**On screen:** the planner, nothing drawn. Four folded steps under the grid.
The invitation on the grid reads **"Nothing drawn yet"**.

> This is your floor. Every square is half a metre — so if you draw the rooms
> at the size they really are, everything that follows means something.
>
> Four steps. Build the floorplan, draw the rooms, tell it which way is
> north, drop the plants in.

**Note:** the app has a built-in walkthrough here — **"Watch me build one"**
— which animates all eleven beats. If the demo is short on time, run that
instead of slides 5 to 12 and narrate over it.

---

## Slide 5 — Step 1, the floorplan image (1:02–1:16)

**On screen:** Step 1 open, a floorplan photo loaded under the grid, opacity
and size sliders being nudged.

> If you have a floorplan — a lettings listing, a survey, a photo of the fire
> notice on the back of the door — drop it in and line it up. It sits under
> the grid as a tracing guide.
>
> It never leaves the phone, and once the rooms are drawn it is deleted.

Or skip it entirely and draw freehand. Say that; it stops the viewer
assuming a floorplan is required.

---

## Slide 6 — Step 2, tracing (1:16–1:32)

**On screen:** dragging a box for the first room, then tapping corner to
corner for an L-shaped one.

> Drag for a box. Tap corner to corner for anything that isn't one. It snaps
> to the grid as you go, so the corners meet.
>
> Rooms that share a wall in life should share an edge here. That is what
> lets light cross between them later.

Open a room; the heading *is* the name field.

> Open a room and the title is the name. Type over it.

---

## Slide 7 — One real size (1:32–1:44)

**On screen:** the ruler tool on the stage, one room's width being typed in.

> Measure one room — one — and the rest scale from it. They are all on the
> same grid, so a single width is enough to make the whole plan true to size.

**Note:** this is the slide people remember, because every other floorplan
tool asks for every room.

---

## Slide 8 — Windows (1:44–2:00)

**On screen:** the room panel, tapping wall segments so they cycle.

> Every wall starts as a wall. Tap it once and it becomes a window. Tap it
> again and it becomes an opening — a doorway, or a missing wall.
>
> A window is the only way daylight gets into a room. An opening lets a room
> borrow light from the room next door, one step dimmer — and only from that
> room's own windows. Light crosses a single doorway. It is never chained
> through the whole flat.

If there is a balcony in the demo plan, tick **"Outdoors?"**:

> Mark a balcony as outdoors and every side with nothing built beyond it
> opens to the sky. No windows to mark.

---

## Slide 9 — Step 3, north (2:00–2:16)

**On screen:** the compass dial, being dragged; the reading ticking in fives.

> A window facing one way is a completely different proposition from the same
> window facing the other. So: which way is north, as you look at the plan.
> A phone compass laid flat on it is the quickest check. The dial settles in
> five-degree steps.

Then the point worth making slowly:

> Until that is confirmed, every room on this plan renders unshaded. No
> guesses, no hedged labels. An app that quietly inverts every aspect in the
> southern hemisphere never looks broken — it is just wrong — so this one
> would rather say nothing until it knows.

---

## Slide 10 — The home view (2:16–2:28)

**On screen:** switch the tab from **"Plan"** to **"Home"**. The isometric
cut-away, floors shaded by light.

> Same rooms, seen from the corner. Each floor is shaded by how much light it
> gets, and you can read the whole flat at a glance — the bright side, the
> dim corner, the room that only gets anything at all through a doorway.

---

## Slide 11 — Step 4, placing a plant (2:28–2:42)

**On screen:** dragging a plant from the tray onto the plan; the floor shades
green and gold beneath it.

> Now drop a plant in. The moment it lands, the whole floor is shaded by how
> well each square suits *that species*. Green is perfect. Gold is fine.
>
> And it tells you the distance — *about two metres from the nearest light* —
> because "by the window" means different things in different rooms.

---

## Slide 12 — A better spot (2:42–2:56)

**On screen:** a plant sitting somewhere mediocre; the panel showing
**"A better spot"** and a **"Move it there"** button.

> If it is in the wrong place, it says so, and it says where instead —
> *happier in the bedroom, by the east window* — with a button that moves it
> there.
>
> If nowhere on the plan suits it, it says that too, rather than picking the
> least bad square and calling it a recommendation.

---

## Slide 13 — Back to the schedule (2:56–3:08)

**On screen:** back to the greenhouse; a room card now carrying a light label
it was never asked for; then a plant page with its watering interval.

> And here is the part that matters. A drawn room's light and aspect are
> never typed — they are read off the plan, every time, and written back onto
> the room.
>
> So the rest of the app never needs to know the planner exists. The
> schedule, the room page, the diagnosis all read that room like any other.
> A window you marked ten minutes ago is now changing how often something
> gets watered.

---

## Slide 14 — What leaves the device (3:08–3:20)

**On screen:** Profile, or the privacy line.

> None of this is uploaded. No account, no server, no telemetry — the plants,
> the photos, the plan and the floorplan image all stay on the phone.
>
> One thing leaves, and it is worth saying plainly rather than rounding up to
> "everything is local": if you set a location, its coordinates go to the
> weather service to fetch a forecast. They are rounded to about a kilometre
> first, so what travels is a neighbourhood rather than an address. Nothing
> else is ever sent.

---

## Slide 15 — Close (3:20–3:30)

**On screen:** Today again, with the plan's rooms now behind it.

> Forty-eight species, each with its own warm and cool season intervals. A
> home read for light rather than guessed at. And one list each morning that
> is short because it is right.

---

## Presenter notes

- **Don't demo an empty app.** Trace the plan before you record, then reset
  only the plant placement — slide 11 lands far better with a real flat under
  it than with two boxes.
- **The hemisphere beat (slide 9) is the credibility moment** for anyone
  technical in the room. It is the one place the app visibly chooses silence
  over a confident wrong answer, and it costs fifteen seconds.
- **Don't say "Sprout" more than twice.** Once to name it, once to close.
- **Numbers that are worth saying out loud:** half a metre per square; five
  rungs of light; five-degree steps on the compass; one room measured, not
  every room; one doorway of borrowed light, never a chain.
- **The single sentence, if you only get one:** *It reads the light in your
  home off a plan you trace in about two minutes, and every watering interval
  in the app is built on that reading.*
